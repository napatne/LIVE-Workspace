## hook · 6s · core

**Figure:** flow — Light → Leaf → Growth

Every plant you have ever seen built itself, mostly out of air and sunlight.

## ingredients · 10s · core

**Heading:** Three ingredients
**Figure:** flow — Sunlight → Water → Carbon dioxide

A plant needs three things. Sunlight on its leaves. Water from its roots. And carbon dioxide,
taken straight out of the air.

## reaction · 8s · core

**Heading:** Inside the leaf
**Figure:** cycle — Sunlight → Water → Sugar → Oxygen

Inside the leaf, sunlight turns that water and carbon dioxide into sugar. The plant keeps the
sugar and releases the oxygen.

## where · 11s · standard

**Heading:** Where it happens
**Figure:** flow — Leaf → Chloroplast → Chlorophyll

It all happens inside chloroplasts — tiny green packets in the leaf. The green you can see is
chlorophyll, the pigment that catches the light.

## scale · 12s · standard

**Heading:** The scale of it
**Figure:** bars — Ocean plankton 50, Forests 30, Grassland 20

This happens on a scale that is hard to picture. Forests and oceans run this same reaction
constantly, everywhere the light reaches. Half the world's oxygen comes from the sea.

## why · 7s · standard

**Heading:** Why it matters
**Figure:** flow — Plants → Oxygen → Animals

That released oxygen is the air we breathe. Almost every animal alive depends on it.

## stages · 14s · extended

**Heading:** Two stages, not one
**Figure:** flow — Light splits water → Energy stored → Carbon locked into sugar

The reaction happens in two stages. First, sunlight splits water apart and releases the
oxygen. Then, in a slower step, the plant uses that stored energy to lock carbon dioxide into
sugar.

## limits · 12s · extended

**Heading:** What slows it down
**Figure:** bars — Light 40, Carbon dioxide 30, Warmth 30

Three things set the pace: how much light there is, how much carbon dioxide, and the
temperature. Whichever is scarcest decides the speed, however much of the others there is.

## respiration · 12s · extended

**Heading:** Plants breathe too
**Figure:** cycle — Oxygen in → Energy → Carbon dioxide out

Plants breathe as well. Day and night they also use oxygen and give off carbon dioxide. In
daylight photosynthesis simply outpaces it, so the leaf gives out far more than it takes.

## discovery · 12s · extended

**Heading:** How we found out
**Figure:** flow — Weigh the soil → Grow the tree → Weigh again

It took three hundred years to work out. Early scientists weighed the soil a tree grew in and
found it had barely lost anything. The mass had come out of the air.

## food-chain · 10s · extended

**Heading:** Everything eats sunlight
**Figure:** flow — Plants → Animals → You

Almost every meal you have ever eaten traces back to this reaction. Plants catch the energy,
and everything else borrows it.

## recap · 3s · core

**Figure:** cycle — Light → Sugar → Oxygen

Light in, sugar and oxygen out.
