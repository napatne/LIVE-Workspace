## hook · 5s · core

**Figure:** crossing — rising "Supply", falling "Demand"

Nobody decides the price of a coffee. It settles.

## demand · 7s · core

**Heading:** Demand
**Figure:** bars — Cheap 55, Medium 32, Expensive 12

When something is cheap, more people want it. Raise the price and fewer do. That is demand.

## supply · 7s · core

**Heading:** Supply
**Figure:** bars — Cheap 12, Medium 33, Expensive 56

Sellers work the other way. The higher the price, the more worth making it is. That is supply.

## equilibrium · 5s · core

**Heading:** Where they meet
**Figure:** crossing — rising "Supply", falling "Demand", point "The price"

Put them together and they cross at one point. That crossing is the price.

## named · 7s · standard

**Heading:** What it's called
**Figure:** crossing — rising "Supply", falling "Demand", point "Equilibrium"

Economists call that crossing point equilibrium — the price where everything on offer gets
sold.

## shift · 11s · standard

**Heading:** When it moves
**Figure:** flow — Less supply → Same demand → Higher price

Change either side and the price moves. A bad harvest cuts supply, so the price rises. A new
fashion lifts demand, and it rises again.

## example · 12s · standard

**Heading:** A real one
**Figure:** flow — Rain → More buyers → Higher price

Think of umbrellas on a rainy day. Demand jumps, supply is the same, and the price goes up —
until more sellers turn up with umbrellas.

## shortage · 12s · extended

**Heading:** Price too low
**Figure:** bars — Wanted 60, Available 20

Set the price below that crossing point and you get a shortage. More people want it than
there is to sell, so queues form, or waiting lists, or it simply sells out by morning.

## surplus · 10s · extended

**Heading:** Price too high
**Figure:** bars — Available 60, Wanted 20

Set it above, and you get the opposite. Goods sit unsold on the shelf, and sellers cut the
price until they move.

## elasticity · 14s · extended

**Heading:** Some things barely budge
**Figure:** bars — Medicine 5, Bread 25, Cinema tickets 70

Not everything responds the same way. People buy roughly the same amount of medicine whether
it is cheap or dear. Other things swing wildly — a small rise in price and the buyers simply
disappear.

## substitutes · 12s · extended

**Heading:** One price moves another
**Figure:** flow — Coffee rises → Buyers switch → Tea sells more

Demand for one thing depends on the price of another. When coffee gets expensive, tea starts
selling better. Economists call those two substitutes.

## limits · 12s · extended

**Heading:** It's a model, not a law
**Figure:** flow — Many buyers → Many sellers → Good information

This is a model, not a law of nature. It assumes plenty of buyers, plenty of sellers, and
everyone knowing roughly what things are worth. Take those away and prices stop behaving.

## recap · 3s · core

**Figure:** crossing — rising "Supply", falling "Demand", point "The price"

Two forces, one crossing point.
