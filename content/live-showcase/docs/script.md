# Script

Every word that appears on screen, in order. The film is silent, so this is the whole of what it
says.

Verified against the scene source, which is where the words actually live — each scene file holds its
own copy as a typed array, so a word changed there and not here would show a difference between this
document and the film.

## The rules this script obeys

**One line on screen at a time.** Where a scene carries more than one, the outgoing line fades before
the next arrives. Scene 6 is the exception: it is a static card, so its elements sit together.

**Signal red marks one word per line, at most.** The palette's red is a pointer, not an emphasis
colour — it lands on the word the line turns on. Everything else is ink, except where a word is
knocked out white on a coloured swatch.

**Lines are set in rows, not paragraphs.** A row is a short group of words that pops together; the
rows stack. It keeps the type large enough to read at speed and gives the stagger something to work
with.

**Nothing is spoken, so nothing is implied.** Anything the audience must understand is on the frame
in words.

## Scene 1 · The ask

| Row | Words | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Do **you** want to make a | ink, with `you` in red |
| 2 | video? | knocked out white, on the periwinkle swatch |

The question is one sentence at one size across both rows. `you` in red is the film pointing at the
viewer in its first line; scene 6 closes by pointing back.

## Scene 2 · The team

| Position | Words | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top | Who's your **team?** | ink, with `team?` in red |
| Bottom | and **you** | ink, with `you` in red |

One sentence split across the frame. The question holds alone at the top while the middle of the
frame answers it three times over, then once — the three branded marks fuse into a single mark, and
that mark becomes the face of the figure carrying `and you`.

## Scene 3 · The storyboard

Two lines, in sequence.

| Line | Rows | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First, upper left | Build a / narrative with / your **agents.** | ink, with `agents.` in red |
| Then, lower left | Then / storyboard **it.** | ink, with `it.` in red |

`Then` rather than `Now`. An imperative immediately after another imperative stops sequencing and
starts raising its voice; `Then` puts the two lines in order without it.

## Scene 4 · Stitching

Four lines, in sequence.

| Line | Words | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over the hand | Superb. | ink |
| Over the working block | Each panel becomes a **scene.** | ink, with `scene.` in red |
| As the strip draws | And stitched together... | ink |
| When it lands | It's a **film!** | ink, with `film!` in red |

The third and fourth lines are one sentence over two beats, with the full-width strip drawing across
the gap between them. The sentence tells the audience what they are about to see rather than asking
them to do anything.

## Scene 5 · Silent era

| Element | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Interface badge | Preview |
| Lane labels | Picture · Sound |
| Intertitle card | Is this a silent film? |
| Cue, as the tool works | Adding sound. |
| Payoff | Yay!! |

**The interface strings are the setup and they are not speech.** `Preview` says the rectangle is
being played rather than hanging on a wall. `Picture` and `Sound` work only as a pair, one lane full
and one named and conspicuously empty. Without them the card lands as a non-sequitur, because nothing
would have told the audience there was an absence to notice.

**The card is a question rather than a statement.** A label has nothing to say about the two
strangest things that just happened — the picture stopped dead and the colour drained out of it, and
the film explains neither. A question is the film noticing, which is what makes the pause and the
wash read as deliberate rather than as a glitch.

**`Adding sound.` rather than an invitation.** By that frame the tool is already laying the band, so
the film is narrating what is happening rather than proposing it. It pairs with the card: a question,
then its answer, neither asking anything of the audience.

## Scene 6 · The LIVE card

| Element | Words | Colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left column | Your turn. | ink |
| Right column | **LIVE** | red |
| Under the wordmark | Learners Intelligent Video Engineer | ink, small |

Plus the QR code, which carries no words and is the one element with a functional requirement.

## The slugs

The two clapboards carry painted slugs. They are the only type in the film not set on paper, and they
are exempt from the paper-fibre treatment for that reason.

| Board | Slug |
| --- | --- |
| Opening, join 1 → 2 | `SCENE 2 · TAKE 1 · LIVE` |
| Closing, join 5 → 6 | `END SCENE 6 · FILM END · LIVE` |

The closing slug is four characters longer than the opening one and the slug row has limited width.
If it stops reading at speed, shorten the wording rather than shrinking the type — a slug that cannot
be read at a glance is doing nothing at any size.

## What is deliberately not copy

These carry meaning without being words, and adding words to them would be redundant:

- **The thumbs-up hand** in scene 1 — the person agreeing to make something.
- **The chef's-kiss hand** in scenes 4 and 6 — approval, and in scene 6 the film still playing
  around after it has finished.
- **The clapping hands** in scene 5 — the payoff `Yay!!` already names.
- **The storyboard sheet's six panels** — drawn, not labelled. They are this film's own scenes.
- **The fusion of three marks into one** in scenes 2 and 4 — the film's whole argument about being
  model-agnostic, made twice, without a sentence either time.
